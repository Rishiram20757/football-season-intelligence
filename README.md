# Football Season Intelligence

Exploratory Data Analysis of Real Football Match Events (StatsBomb Open Data)

## Overview

**Football Season Intelligence** is a portfolio project analysing one complete football season
using real event data. The notebook `notebooks/01_football_season_intelligence.ipynb` performs
a football-driven exploratory analysis of the **English Premier League 2015/2016** — all 380
matches, every match file and every event file processed from the official StatsBomb Open Data
repository.

Reusable analysis code lives in `src/` (`data_loader`, `data_validation`, `football_metrics`,
`visualizations`); the notebook tells the analytical story end to end.

## Football problem statement

What can we learn about a football season from match-event data alone? The notebook investigates
ten questions an analytics department would actually ask:

1. How are goals distributed across matches?
2. Which teams score the most and concede the fewest goals?
3. Do teams with higher shot volume also create better-quality chances?
4. How does shot location relate to shot outcomes?
5. Which players record the most shots, goals, and expected goals?
6. Which teams accumulate the most passing activity?
7. How do passing completion rates vary between teams?
8. How does performance differ between first and second halves?
9. What is the relationship between expected goals and actual goals?
10. What can — and cannot — be inferred from this dataset?

## Dataset and selection

**Source:** StatsBomb Open Data — https://github.com/statsbomb/open-data
(CC BY 4.0-compatible public release; see the repository README/license for attribution
requirements).

**Selected competition-season:** England — Premier League — 2015/2016
(`competition_id: 2`, `season_id: 27`).

**Why this season:** it is a complete modern season — 380 matches, 20 teams, match weeks
1–38, dates 2015-08-08 to 2016-05-17 — for which every match file and every event file is
available in the open-data repository (380/380 matches and events processed). Tottenham
Hotspur played all 38 of its matches in this season (the club of interest for this project).

**Coverage:** all 380 match metadata files and all 380 event files were downloaded and
processed successfully. Data is cached locally under `data/raw/` so re-runs do not re-download;
raw files are not committed to the repository (see `.gitignore`).

## Key findings (computed from the executed analysis)

- **Scoring:** 1,026 goals in 380 matches (2.70 per match, median 3); 3 goals was the most
  common match total.
- **Home advantage (descriptive):** home teams scored 567 vs 459 away goals; home win share
  41.3%, draws 28.2%, away wins 30.5%. Home scored 0.284 more goals per match on average
  (bootstrap 95% CI: 0.110 to 0.458) — descriptive for this season only.
- **Attack/defence:** Leicester City scored the most goals (67); Tottenham Hotspur conceded
  the fewest (32). Tottenham finished 3rd on derived points (70: 19W–13D–6L), with 68 goals
  for and a +36 goal difference.
- **Shots:** 9,908 shots for 988 goals (10.0% conversion); 32.3% of shots on target; 848 of
  988 goals came from inside/near the penalty area.
- **Volume vs quality:** team shots vs goals correlate at r = 0.895 — more shots generally
  meant more goals, but conversion differed between teams.
- **Expected goals:** season xG total 971.4 vs 1,026 actual goals; team xG vs actual goals
  correlate at r = 0.954. xG is StatsBomb's `statsbomb_xg` estimate; no xG model was trained.
- **Players:** Harry Kane led with 25 goals from 158 shots (xG 21.64), ahead of Jamie Vardy
  and Sergio Agüero (24 each).
- **Passing:** 368,619 passes; Arsenal had the highest completion rate (83.2%), Tottenham
  79.2% on 20,624 attempted passes (3rd-highest volume). High completion ≠ tactical
  superiority by itself.
- **Halves:** 547 of 1,026 goals (53.3%) were scored in the second half.

## Data limitations

- Single season (Premier League 2015/2016); descriptive, not generalising.
- Shot-derived goals matched the official scoreboard in 342 of 380 matches; the remaining 38
  differed by small margins (event-data nuance). Match results use the official scoreboard;
  shooting analysis uses shot events, and this is reported transparently in the notebook.
- Event data records on-ball actions only; off-ball positioning and full tactical shape are
  not observed.
- xG is an estimate (`statsbomb_xg`), not ground truth; no xG model is trained here.
- Minutes played are derived from lineup position intervals and treated as approximate;
  per-90 rates are not headline metrics.
- Raw totals are not adjusted for opponent strength or game state; correlation is not
  causation.

## Heung-Min Son: Player Intelligence Case Study

### Objective and research questions

A focused, data-driven investigation of Heung-Min Son's attacking performance for Tottenham Hotspur in the English Premier League 2015/2016 season, using verified StatsBomb Open Data events. The companion notebook is `notebooks/02_heung_min_son_player_analysis.ipynb`.

Research questions:
1. Where does Son generate his shots and attacking actions on the pitch?
2. How does his shot quality (xG) compare with his actual goal output?
3. What kinds of actions (shots, passes, carries, dribbles) contribute to his attacking involvement?
4. How does his per-90 attacking profile compare with other Premier League attackers?
5. What can — and cannot — be concluded from this sample?

### Competition and season

English Premier League 2015/2016 (`competition_id: 2`, `season_id: 27`) — the same season analysed in Project 1. All 380 matches and event files were processed.

### Key findings (computed from the executed analysis)

- **Shots:** 38 shots, 4 goals (10.5% conversion), 13 shots on target (34.2%)
- **Expected goals:** Total xG 3.85, xG per shot 0.10, goals minus xG +0.15 (scored essentially in line with expected quality)
- **Passing:** 455 passes, 359 completed (78.9% completion), 1 goal assist, 17 shot assists
- **Other actions:** 469 carries, 49 dribbles
- **Per 90 minutes:** 3.10 shots per 90, 0.33 goals per 90, 0.31 xG per 90
- **Comparison:** Among 349 Premier League attackers with >= 5 shots and minutes data, Son ranks 52nd for goals per 90 and 46th for xG per 90
